Work through three checks after you integrate an SDK or install ZeroGPU Edge: the device is **connected**, it's **eligible** for work, and it's **earning**.

## 1. Connected

The device has joined the network with your SDK key.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Android">
    Construct the SDK with `enableLogs = true`, keep the app in the foreground, and watch logcat:

    ```bash
    adb logcat -s ZeroGpuSdk:* ZeroGpuWebSocket:*
    ```

    The device is connected once you see `WebSocket ready for tasks`.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Chrome / Web / Telegram">
    Turn on console logs (`enableConsoleLogs="true"` on the script tag, or `enableConsoleLogs: true` in `offscreen.js`) and open the console. For a Chrome extension, that's **Inspect views: offscreen.html** on `chrome://extensions`. The SDK logs the device registering, loading a model, and becoming ready.

    On a website, the handle returned by `initZeroGpuSdk` reports `status === "running"`. For an extension, `npx zerogpu doctor` checks the integration itself.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="macOS app">
    ```js
    await worker.status(); // { running: true, ready: true, ... }
    ```

    `ready: true` means a model is loaded and the Mac is connected.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Fleet">
    ```bash
    zerogpu-edge status
    ```

    The status shows the service registered and serving. The ZeroGPU Edge app shows the same under **Status**. If it doesn't, run `zerogpu-edge doctor`.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

Then open **Analytics** in the [Edge Operator Portal](https://edge.zerogpu.ai). The **Device Fleet** map shows devices seen in the last 24 hours, and **Unique Devices** counts them over time.

## 2. Eligible for workloads

A connected device receives work when all of these hold:

| Check | Where to look |
|---|---|
| The app or fleet is **Approved**, not Pending Review, Suspended, or Rejected | **My Apps** in the portal |
| The platform you integrated is one of the app types or device types you registered | **My Apps** → your app's setup |
| The device has the resources to run a model: free memory and storage, and on phones, a battery above 20% or charging | SDK logs (`degraded` events), `zerogpu-edge doctor` |
| It's allowed to run right now: the app is open (mobile and browser), the Mac is on power (macOS app SDK policy), or the fleet machine is inside its `--resource-policy` window | Your integration, `zerogpu-edge status` |
| The user hasn't turned resource sharing off | Your opt-out setting |

<Note>
  A registration that's "politely refused", or a device that sits ready without tasks, is normal. Work follows network demand, and new devices get less work at first. Keep devices running, and keep their stored identity (app data, `ZG_DATA_DIR`, or the fleet's state folder): a reset device starts over as a newcomer.
</Note>

## 3. Earning

On **Analytics** in the portal, for each app or across all of them:

- **Edge Requests** counts the requests your devices served.
- **Earnings** shows what those requests earned. You earn a revenue share of every request your devices serve.
- Filter by app and date range. Earnings broken down by reporting label (`cust_param1`–`cust_param3`) are available through the Supply Analytics API; contact [hello@zerogpu.ai](mailto:hello@zerogpu.ai) for access.

On **My Apps**, an app or fleet that is serving traffic shows as **Active/Earning**. Withdraw your balance from **Withdrawals**.

<Tip>
  Analytics can lag real time by a few minutes. If devices show as connected but earnings stay at zero for more than a day, contact [hello@zerogpu.ai](mailto:hello@zerogpu.ai) with your app name and the platform.
</Tip>
